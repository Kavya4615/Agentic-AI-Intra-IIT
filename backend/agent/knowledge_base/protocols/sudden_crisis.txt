# Pneumothorax / Sudden Crisis Protocol (BTS Guidelines)

1. DIAGNOSIS
- Sudden onset dyspnoea, tachycardia, and desaturation are cardinal signs.
- Primary spontaneous: occurs without underlying lung disease.
- Secondary: occurs with pre-existing lung disease (higher mortality risk).
- Tension pneumothorax: tracheal deviation, absent breath sounds, haemodynamic collapse — life-threatening emergency.

2. IMMEDIATE MANAGEMENT (Tension Pneumothorax)
- Immediate needle decompression: second intercostal space, mid-clavicular line.
- Follow with chest drain insertion (4th or 5th ICS, mid-axillary line).
- Do NOT delay for imaging in haemodynamically unstable patient.

3. OXYGEN AND RESUSCITATION
- High-flow oxygen 10-15 L/min via non-rebreather mask.
- Oxygen accelerates reabsorption of pneumothorax (up to 4x faster).
- Target SpO2 > 94% (or 88-92% if risk of hypercapnia).
- IV access and fluid resuscitation if haemodynamically compromised.

4. MONITORING
- Continuous SpO2 and HR monitoring essential. Acute desaturation (SpO2 < 90%) combined with tachycardia (HR > 120) is a critical alert pattern.
- Serial CXR every 2-4 hours to assess re-expansion.
